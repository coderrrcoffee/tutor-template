# Tutor Website Skill

## Goal

Create websites that help a private tutor present their expertise and convert visitors into inquiries.

The site should communicate:

- who the tutor is
- who they teach
- what they teach
- why students choose them
- what results or experience they have
- how lessons work
- how to contact them

## Typical structure

A small tutor website may contain:

1. Home
2. About
3. Lessons / Services
4. Results
5. Reviews
6. FAQ
7. Contact

Not every project needs every page.

Choose the structure based on the tutor's actual information.

## Hero

The first screen should quickly communicate:

- subject
- target audience
- tutor identity
- main benefit
- primary action

Avoid vague hero copy.

Instead of generic phrases like:

"Learn better. Achieve more."

Prefer specific positioning.

Example:

"English lessons for teenagers who want to speak confidently – without boring textbooks."

## Trust

Use real evidence when available:

- education
- certifications
- teaching experience
- student results
- reviews
- competition/exam results
- years of experience

Never invent credentials, statistics or testimonials.

## Reviews

Reviews should be easy to scan.

When real reviews are provided, preserve their meaning and do not fabricate additional ones.

## Conversion

The primary CTA should be clear and repeated naturally.

Examples:

- Book a trial lesson
- Ask a question
- Write on Telegram
- Schedule a consultation

Avoid excessive CTA buttons.

## Tone

The website should feel:

- human
- competent
- trustworthy
- approachable
- specific

Avoid corporate language unless the tutor's positioning requires it.

## Content

Never invent factual claims about the tutor.

If information is missing, use clearly marked placeholders or ask for the missing information.

## Differentiation

Every tutor site should have its own visual identity.

Do not reuse the same layout blindly.

Adapt:

- typography
- color palette
- imagery
- composition
- tone
- content hierarchy

to the tutor.
